# TÂM TRÍ — Video Design System

> **COFFAT MEDIA · Channel Design Specification**  
> Version 1.0 · Format: vertical psychology / human-behavior documentary · Aspect ratio: 9:16

## 1. Purpose and scope

TÂM TRÍ explores psychology, human behavior, memory, attention, cognition, emotion, relationships, decision-making, and neuroscience.

This document defines the channel's visual system for vertical videos: human-centered footage, editorial typography, explanatory diagrams, subtitles, transitions, and reusable Hyperframe compositions.

**This is a video design system only.** Do not use it to generate a website, app, dashboard, social banner, avatar, or thumbnail.

### Design direction

- **Primary Style:** **Style 4 + Humanist Editorial — Kinetic Thought Typography & Authentic B-roll** (Cognitive Rhythm & Real Human Emotion) × Psychology Magazine × Swiss Minimalism.
- **Mood:** thoughtful, empathetic, grounded, intimate, emotionally resonant, reflective, and non-judgmental.
- **Visual thesis:** human psychology is rooted in authentic lived experiences and internal cognitive rhythms—not glowing digital brains, lightning synapses, or robotic emoji faces.
- **Primary principle:** word-synchronized thought streams paced with the natural cadence of narration, generous visual breathing room, and candid real human B-roll footage capturing subtle emotional nuance.

The channel should feel like a deeply personal, beautifully art-directed psychology essay. It permanently eliminates generic 3D brains, digital synapse lightning, and uncanny AI human faces with exaggerated fake emotions.

## 2. Core visual principles

1. **Authentic human presence over artificial symbols.** Showcase real candid human B-roll—micro-expressions, subtle gestures, thoughtful pauses, natural interactions in real environments.
2. **Kinetic Thought Typography paced to cognitive rhythm.** Present ideas as synchronized, breathing typography streams where key thoughts animate in precise sync with the voiceover cadence, guiding introspection.
3. **Generous visual breathing room.** Embrace ample negative space on warm white `paper` (`#FAFAF7`) and soft `dusty-rose` (`#E9DED8`) fields. Never crowd or rush the viewer's psychological contemplation.
4. **Natural color fidelity and warmth.** Ground the experience in human warmth using `muted-plum` (`#795C76`) for key concepts, `terracotta` (`#C47C70`) for emotional turning points, and authentic human skin tones.
5. **Empathetic framing, not clinical diagnosis.** Visual treatments must illuminate cognitive patterns and emotional nuances without stigmatizing or turning normal human struggles into pathological caricatures.
6. **Strictly genuine candid humans over uncanny AI.** Strictly ban generative AI portraits, exaggerated actor grimaces, or plastic-skinned stock models pretending to experience genuine emotion.
7. **Distinct boundaries between theory, metaphor, and evidence.** Clarify psychological research caveats, separating cognitive metaphors from literal neurological mechanisms.

- Use `muted-plum` (`#795C76`) for the central psychological insight or kinetic thought word.
- Use `terracotta` (`#C47C70`) sparingly for emotional tension or behavioral contrast.
- Preserve 100% natural skin tones and organic lighting in all human B-roll footage; never tint human faces with brand dyes.
- **Strict Anti-AI Palette Guardrail:** Absolutely ban neon purple-cyan synapse gradients, glowing electric pinks, or depressive overly desaturated horror filters.

## 4. Typography

### Recommended families

| Purpose | Preferred | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Main editorial headline | Lora | Source Serif 4 |
| Body and explanatory text | Inter | Manrope |
| Secondary labels and diagram text | Inter / Manrope | IBM Plex Sans |
| Study data and compact values | IBM Plex Mono | Roboto Mono |

The serif headline adds a human, reflective voice. Sans-serif supports clarity and neutrality. Mono is reserved for study values, dates, and compact technical information.

### Vertical-video type scale (1080 × 1920 reference)

| Role | Starting size | Weight | Guidance |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Hero headline | 88–112 px | 600–700 | Reflective, clear, 2–4 lines |
| Key statement | 62–80 px | 600–700 | A question, misconception, or insight |
| Body explanation | 38–48 px | 400–500 | Plain language, short phrases |
| Diagram label | 28–36 px | 450–600 | A few words per label |
| Study / metadata | 22–28 px | 500–600 | Researcher, year, measure, source |
| Subtitles | 44–54 px | 500–600 | Maximum 2 lines |

### Typography rules

- Prefer natural sentence case for human-centered messages.
- Avoid all-caps emotional declarations and overly bold motivational typography.
- Keep display line-height around `0.98–1.12` and explanatory line-height around `1.3–1.5`.
- Do not place long research summaries in small text. Break them into separate scenes.
- Ensure full Vietnamese diacritics and punctuation render correctly.

## 5. Canvas, composition, and safe area

- **Master canvas:** `1080 × 1920 px`, portrait `9:16`.
- Keep essential text, diagrams, labels, and key facial context approximately 90 px from left/right, 140 px from top, and 300 px from bottom.
- Recheck platform-specific interface overlays before export.
- Use breathing room around people and text. Avoid placing headline text across eyes, facial expressions, or meaningful gestures.
- Prefer a human subject in one zone and a concise headline or annotation in another.
- Use full-bleed human footage when it communicates the experience more honestly than a graphic.
- Use centered typography only for a deliberate reflective statement, not as the default for every scene.

## 6. Human imagery and candid B-roll direction

### Preferred material

- Authentic candid human B-roll: real people in unstaged everyday moments—reading on a train, contemplating at a desk, looking out a rainy window, gentle smiles, listening intently in conversation.
- Natural human micro-details: hands writing in a journal, coffee steam, a shift in gaze, micro-expressions of curiosity or hesitation.
- Organic interior and exterior environments: sunlit libraries, quiet cafes, urban pedestrian paths, open parks with natural ambient lighting.
- Thoughtful, respectful framing with authentic cinematic depth of field and true-to-life skin tones.
- Credible psychological research environments: actual scientific journals, cognitive experiment tasks, and historical laboratory notes.

### Image treatment

- Natural, warm color grading with soft contrast. Avoid high-saturation music-video grades or muddy desaturated horror palettes.
- Candid, respectful observation over theatrical performance: allow the subject's humanity and dignity to resonate.
- Framed with wide negative space to leave breathing room for kinetic typography without crowding the subject's eyeline.
- Never portray a single candid reaction as clinical diagnostic proof.

### Strict Anti-AI Guardrails (Do Not Use)

- **Glowing 3D brains & digital synapses:** Absolutely ban rotating translucent 3D brains, glowing electric neuron webs, or lightning bolts firing between hemispheres.
- **Uncanny AI human faces:** Never use Midjourney/generative portraits with waxy plastic skin, dead eyes, six fingers, or exaggerated fake emotional states.
- **Overdramatic stock stereotypes:** Sappy stock actors clutching their temples in fake agony, melodramatic crying in corners, or screaming at office laptops.
- **Emoji & cartoon psychology:** Floating smiley/frown emojis, cartoon lightbulbs over heads, or pop-psychology comic bubbles.

## 7. Kinetic Thought Typography & Humanist Editorial language

- **Style 4 Signature — Kinetic Thought Typography:** Present psychological propositions as word-synchronized thought streams. Words or short phrases materialize in rhythm with the narrator's vocal inflection, reflecting internal cognitive processing.
- **Cognitive Cadence & Breathing Room:** Generous line-height (`1.4–1.6`) and expansive whitespace around phrases, creating calm cognitive space for reflection.
- **Subtle Thought Anchors:** Key psychological terms rendered in `Lora` / `Source Serif 4` italic or highlighted in `muted-plum` (`#795C76`); emotional turning points marked by `terracotta` (`#C47C70`).
- **Minimal Cognitive Process Diagrams:** Simple relational flows (stimulus → appraisal → response) using fine `ink` rules and restrained text cards on soft `dusty-rose` (`#E9DED8`) containers.
- **Research Citation Cards:** Clean, understated footnotes for empirical studies (author, university, year) positioned quietly at the lower safe boundary.

## 8. Motion and transitions

Motion in Style 4 mimics the gentle, introspective rhythm of a contemplative human thought.

### Preferred motion

- **Word-Synchronized Thought Stream:** Words reveal with soft opacity fades (`opacity: 0 -> 1`) and gentle upward drift (`y: 8px -> 0px`, `ease: "power2.out"`) matched tightly to the narrator's audio pauses.
- **Slow Natural B-roll Drift:** Unhurried, handheld-style subtle pan or slow push-in (`1.0 -> 1.03` over 4–6s) on authentic human subjects.
- **Thought Pause & Hold:** Extended holds (1.5s–2.5s) on critical reflective questions to allow deep cognitive digestion before cutting.
- **Soft Editorial Cross-fades:** Smooth, gentle dissolves (`0.6s–0.8s`) between candid human moments and minimalist typography frames.

### Motion constraints

- Strictly avoid kinetic hype-style typography (rapid flash-word-per-frame TikTok cuts, aggressive zooms, screen shakes).
- Strictly avoid glitch effects, digital wipes, lightning pulses, or strobe flickers.
- Motion must calm and ground the viewer, never induce sensory anxiety or manic arousal.
- The static resting frame must stand as an elegant, publication-grade editorial spread.

## 9. Subtitle and on-screen text system

- Use natural sentence-level subtitles, maximum two lines, respecting grammatical clauses.
- Default to dark `ink` text on a warm-white plate over busy footage; white text on dark plate (`#262126`) over low-key introspective scenes.
- Highlight at most one reflective term or cognitive keyword per subtitle in `muted-plum` (`#795C76`); use `terracotta` (`#C47C70`) strictly for emotional contrast.
- Never place subtitles across a subject's face, eyes, or expressive hand gestures.
- Maintain consistent placement in the lower safe area with gentle fade transitions.

## 10. Reusable scene patterns

1. **Recognizable moment:** start with a familiar human situation or thought.
2. **Question:** state the psychological question without diagnosing the viewer.
3. **Concept:** introduce the term or mechanism using clear editorial typography.
4. **Explanation:** show a simple relationship diagram or grounded metaphor.
5. **Evidence and nuance:** present the research finding, limitation, or alternative explanation.
6. **Reflection:** end with a useful reframing or question, not a generic motivational slogan.

Use only the scenes needed for each story. Preserve room for nuance; not every psychology topic should follow a “problem → trick → solution” formula.

## 11. Evidence, ethics, and source handling

- Do not diagnose viewers or people shown in footage.
- Distinguish research findings, theories, hypotheses, anecdotes, and simplified explanatory models.
- Do not imply that correlation proves causation.
- Preserve caveats about sample size, study context, and limitations when they change the meaning of a claim.
- Avoid medical or therapeutic promises unless the script is explicitly supported by qualified sources and appropriate context.
- Do not frame normal variation in behavior as a disorder.
- Use respectful, non-stigmatizing language and imagery.

## 12. Anti-patterns

- **Glowing 3D brains & synapse lightning:** Translucent holographic brain models, neon synaptic firing arcs, and cybernetic nervous system renderings.
- **Uncanny AI human faces:** Midjourney/generative portraits with artificial plastic skin, glassy dead eyes, or cartoonish fake tears.
- **Melodramatic stock acting:** Clichéd stock actors screaming in frustration, clutching their heads in agony, or posing in contrived clinical depression tropes.
- **Social-media hype typography:** Flashing single-word captions bouncing frantically every 0.2s, giant red warning arrows, or sensationalist emoji stickers.
- **Overly diagnostic labeling:** Framing complex human experiences into simplistic pathological diagnoses or toxic self-help clichés.
- **Visual claustrophobia:** Cramming too many paragraphs or cards into one frame, destroying the contemplative negative space.

## 13. Hyperframe implementation guidance

- Create reusable **humanist editorial compositions**: candid human B-roll hero, word-synchronized kinetic thought stream, contemplative question card, relational cognitive model, empirical study footnote card, and reflective closing insight.
- Structure layouts with generous negative space, soft background fields (`dusty-rose` `#E9DED8`), and editorial typographic hierarchy (`Lora` / `Source Serif 4` + `Inter`).
- Tokenize `paper` (`#FAFAF7`), `ink` (`#292526`), `muted-plum` (`#795C76`), `terracotta` (`#C47C70`), `dusty-rose` (`#E9DED8`), and `rule` (`#E7E2DF`).
- Calibrate GSAP text reveals for word-by-word opacity and gentle vertical drift, synchronized to VieNeu-TTS `Mai Anh` audio pauses.
- Keep footage unaltered: allow real human expressions to play full-bleed with minimal letterboxed editorial plates.
- Never bake typography into raster video backgrounds.
